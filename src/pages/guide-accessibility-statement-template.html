Free accessibility statement template for your online store

Copy it, fill in the brackets, and publish it in your footer

An accessibility statement is a short public page that says what accessibility standard your store is working toward, what you have done, what you know still needs work, and how a customer can reach you if something doesn't work for them. It doesn't make a store compliant on its own, but it gives shoppers a way to get help and gives you a dated, public record of good-faith effort. Below is a template you can use for free.

1.The template

Replace everything in [square brackets]. Delete any line that isn't true for your store.

Accessibility statement for [Store name]

[Store name] wants everyone to be able to shop with us, including people who use a screen reader, a keyboard, magnification or other assistive technology.

Our standard. We are working to make [website address] meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at Level AA, the standard most widely used for websites in the United States.

What we have done. As of [month and year], we have [for example: had our store audited, fixed the issues found in our theme code, and set up ongoing checks so new products and apps don't introduce new problems]. [If a person has tested your store with a keyboard and a screen reader, say so here and give the date.]

What we know still needs work. [List anything you know is not yet accessible, for example: some older product videos don't have captions yet, and we expect to add them by month and year. If you know of nothing, say: We are not aware of any outstanding issues, but we test regularly and welcome reports.]

Third-party content. Some features on our site come from other companies, such as [reviews, chat or payment tools]. We choose providers with accessibility in mind and report problems to them when we find them.

Contact us. If you have trouble using any part of our store, email [email address] or call [phone number]. Tell us the page and what happened, and we will help you complete your order another way while we fix it. We aim to reply within [two business days].

This statement was last reviewed on [date].

2.Say only what you can stand behind

The most common mistake is claiming more than the work supports. Words like "fully compliant," "ADA certified" or "100% accessible" are claims a plaintiff's firm can test, and there is no official ADA certification for websites to back them up. Saying you are working to meet WCAG 2.2 Level AA is accurate for most stores.

Say your store conforms to WCAG 2.2 Level AA only if the whole site has been tested against every Level A and AA requirement, including by a person using a keyboard and a screen reader, and the issues found were fixed. An automated scan alone, including our free accessibility check, can't support that claim, because automated tools catch only part of the problems.

Don't name an accessibility widget or overlay as the reason your store is accessible. Regulators have acted against exactly that kind of claim (see why a widget can't protect you).

3.Where to publish it

Put the statement on its own page and link to it from your site footer, so it is one click from every page. Name the link "Accessibility" so people can find it.

On Shopify, go to Online Store, then Pages, and add a page called Accessibility. Then go to Online Store, then Navigation, open your footer menu and add a link to the new page. On WordPress or WooCommerce, add a page under Pages, then add it to your footer menu in the menu or site editor. On BigCommerce, other platforms and custom-coded sites it works the same way: a standard page, linked in the footer.

4.Keep it current

An out-of-date statement works against you. Review it whenever you change themes, add a major app or fix a known issue, and at least once a year, and update the "last reviewed" date each time. Make sure the email address and phone number in it are monitored, and answer requests quickly: a customer who gets help through your statement has less reason to look for a lawyer.

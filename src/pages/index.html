Isn't that just a VPAT?

No, and the difference matters. A VPAT (Voluntary Product Accessibility Template) is a standard form that software vendors fill in to tell buyers how their product performs against WCAG today. The completed form is called an Accessibility Conformance Report. It was designed for universities and government agencies comparing software, and it answers one question: where does this product stand right now?

That is useful, which is why the Record includes one. But a VPAT is a snapshot with no history, no proof you kept going, no human testing, and no plan for the day a letter lands. A plaintiff's firm can read one and still argue you only acted after they contacted you. The Record is built to answer that argument.

WCAG 2.2 EXPLAINED

The standard, in plain English.

WCAG 2.2 is the W3C's web accessibility standard: 86 success criteria across three levels. The 55 at Levels A and AA are the target referenced by ADA guidance and settlement agreements, and the ones we score and fix.

Every criterion falls under one of four principles, known as POUR. Meet all four and your store works for the screen reader user, the keyboard-only user, the colorblind user, and Google's crawler.

  • P

    Perceivable

    Alt text, captions, color contrast — content users can see or hear.
  • O

    Operable

    Full keyboard navigation, no traps, generous tap targets.
  • U

    Understandable

    Clear labels, predictable patterns, helpful error messages.
  • R

    Robust

    Semantic HTML and ARIA so assistive tech reads it correctly.
AA
Target Level AA. Level A is the floor (and not enough). Level AAA is aspirational and unachievable for most ecommerce content. DOJ guidance and settlement agreements reference AA.

A warning about accessibility plug-ins.

If you have installed an accessibility plug-in that promises instant compliance, it does not work. A plug-in loads a script on top of your store and leaves the code underneath exactly as it was, so a shopper using a screen reader or a keyboard hits the same barriers. About one in four ADA digital accessibility lawsuits in 2024 targeted a site running one (UsableNet), and in 2025 the FTC ordered accessiBe, the best-known plug-in vendor, to pay $1 million over claims about what its product could do. Plaintiffs' firms now read a plug-in as a sign that nothing was fixed. Accessrank fixes the source.

FAQ

Questions worth answering honestly.

Is a Shopify store automatically ADA compliant?

No. Shopify provides a well-built foundation, but it does not warrant that any store meets accessibility standards. Shopify's own published Accessibility Conformance Reports describe its checkout and default Dawn theme as “partially supporting” several WCAG Level AA criteria, including color contrast and visible focus, and Shopify expressly disclaims any warranty regarding those reports.

Everything a merchant adds on top of the platform, including custom themes, design changes and third-party apps, is the merchant's responsibility. Apps are not reviewed for accessibility as a condition of listing in the Shopify App Store. Independent research by WebAIM found that in 2026, Shopify home pages in its sample averaged more detectable accessibility errors than the overall average of the top one million websites.

Accessibility “overlay” widgets do not change this, since they do not alter the underlying code. The U.S. Federal Trade Commission has taken enforcement action against unsubstantiated claims that such tools make websites compliant.

Accessrank identifies barriers in your store's actual theme and app code, remediates them at the source, and documents the work. That is the approach courts and settlement agreements consistently look for.

Is an accessibility overlay enough?

No. An overlay is a script that loads on top of your store and tries to patch problems while the page is displayed. It leaves the underlying code exactly as it was, so a screen reader or keyboard user hits the same barriers, and plaintiffs' firms have learned to look for overlay scripts as a sign that nothing was actually fixed. In 2024, more than 1,000 of the 4,187 ADA digital accessibility lawsuits filed, about one in four, targeted sites that were running an overlay or widget (UsableNet).

In 2025 the Federal Trade Commission ordered overlay vendor accessiBe to pay $1 million to settle charges that it misrepresented its product's ability to make websites compliant with WCAG.

Accessrank does not install a widget. We fix the HTML, CSS and ARIA in your theme and app code, so the improvement is in the store itself.

Your report also shows two separate numbers beside it: an on-page SEO score and an AI readiness score, which measures how well shopping agents and AI search tools can read your store. Who actually does the remediation work?

Accessrank fixes every finding in your theme code, and most of it within the first remediation cycle. Our remediation tooling handles the fixes that follow clear rules, such as labels, structure, focus handling and markup.

The findings that need a judgment call, such as writing or reviewing alt text, describing media and deciding how a control should behave, are done and reviewed by an Accessrank staff member. You get a record of what changed and why.

Will this change how my store looks?

Almost never in a way shoppers would notice. The large majority of fixes are invisible: labels for screen readers, heading structure, keyboard focus handling, form markup and alt text.

The one visible exception is color contrast. If your brand palette uses text that is too light against its background, we propose the smallest adjustment that passes and agree it with you before anything goes live.

What is WCAG 2.2 Level AA?

WCAG (the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) is the international checklist for whether a website works for people with disabilities. It is published by the W3C, the body that sets web standards, and it is the yardstick US courts, the Department of Justice and settlement agreements use when they decide whether a site meets the ADA.

It has three levels. Level A is the minimum and is not enough on its own. Level AA is the middle level and the one legal guidance references. Level AAA is aspirational and largely unachievable for an ecommerce catalog, so we do not test against it. We score and fix against the 55 Level A and AA checks, the legally relevant ones.

Does the free check prove I am compliant?

No, and be wary of any tool that claims it does. Automated testing, including our free check, catches roughly a third to a half of accessibility barriers. It is a fast way to surface obvious issues, but full WCAG conformance also requires manual testing with a keyboard and a screen reader, which is what our paid audits and remediation include.

Our ongoing monitoring works the same way: it watches your store for the problems that can be machine-checked, so a new app, theme edit or product upload does not quietly undo the work. The manual pass happens at onboarding and in each remediation cycle.

Do I qualify for the Form 8826 tax credit?

Many DTC stores do. The IRS Disabled Access Credit (Form 8826) lets eligible small businesses claim 50% of qualifying accessibility expenses between $250 and $10,250 in a year, up to $5,000 back against your tax bill.

To be eligible, your business needs either $1 million or less in gross receipts, or 30 or fewer full-time employees, in the preceding tax year. The credit is non-refundable and subject to IRS rules. Accessrank is not a tax advisor and this is not tax advice, so confirm your eligibility with a qualified professional.

Does Accessrank cover European requirements (the EAA)?

No. Accessrank's services are built around US standards: the ADA and WCAG 2.2 Level AA. We do not currently offer European Accessibility Act conformance work or make any claims about EU compliance. The code-level fixes we make are the same ones any accessibility standard asks for, but if you need documented EAA conformance you will want a specialist in that market.

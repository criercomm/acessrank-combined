How to get your products recommended by ChatGPT and AI shopping assistants

What AI assistants read about your products, and how to improve it

More shoppers now start by asking an AI assistant, "What's the best moisturizer for dry skin under $40?", instead of searching. ChatGPT, Google's AI Mode and Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and Perplexity all answer with specific products. Which products they name depends largely on the information they can find and read about them. This page explains where that information comes from and what you can do about it.

1.How do AI assistants choose which products to recommend?

No assistant publishes a full formula, but they have said what they weigh. OpenAI says ChatGPT considers the shopper's request and context, along with things like price, reviews and availability, and that its product results are not ads. To do that, an assistant needs clear, consistent facts about each product: what it is, who it's for, what it costs, whether it's in stock, and what other buyers say about it.

Those facts come from product feeds that merchants or their platforms send, from your own product pages, and from what the wider web says about your brand. When the facts are thin, vague or inconsistent, an assistant has little reason to choose your product over one it understands better.

2.Does my store platform send my products to AI assistants?

Shopify sends eligible stores' product data to AI assistants through Shopify Catalog and a feature it calls Agentic Storefronts, which brought Shopify products into ChatGPT in March 2026, with other assistants following. For most US Shopify stores this is on by default, and you can manage it under Sales channels in your Shopify admin.

On WooCommerce, BigCommerce, WordPress or a custom-coded site, the main route is a product feed you control. Google’s assistants draw on Google Merchant Center, and OpenAI accepts direct product feeds from merchants who apply for them. Your product pages themselves, and the structured product data in their code, are read either way.

Being included is not the same as being recommended. The feed carries the product data you already have, so short titles, missing details and empty fields travel with it.

3.What should I improve?

  • Product titles that say what the product is, not just its name. "Juniper Hydrating Face Cream for Dry Skin, 50 ml" tells an assistant far more than "Juniper."
  • Descriptions that answer the questions shoppers ask: what it's made of, who it suits, sizes, how to use it, how it compares.
  • Complete product details: brand, product type, category, materials, size and color options, price and stock status, filled in the fields your platform provides rather than only in the description.
  • Image descriptions for every product photo, so the image tells an assistant what the product looks like.
  • Reviews and ratings that are visible on the product page and included in the page's product data.
  • Store policies for shipping and returns that are published and easy to find.
  • A Google Merchant Center feed that is complete and free of errors, since Google's assistants draw on it.
  • Consistent facts across the web. Your brand name, product names and prices should match on your site, marketplaces and anywhere else they appear.

4.Where does accessibility fit in?

Assistants and search engines read your store's code, not its looks, much like a screen reader does. Accessibility fixes such as image descriptions, a clear heading structure, labeled buttons and real text instead of text in images make your pages easier for them to read. Accessibility fixes lift your SEO and AI readiness. Product data decides whether assistants recommend you. We do both.

5.How can I tell if it's working?

Ask the assistants the questions your customers would ask, such as "best [product type] for [need] under [price]," and note whether your brand appears. Repeat the same questions every month or two, since answers change over time. On Shopify, orders that come from ChatGPT are marked with a referral source in your admin. On any platform, your analytics will show visits referred by AI assistants such as chatgpt.com.

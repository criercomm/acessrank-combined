Why a widget can't protect you

A widget is a script added on top of your pages. The problems live in the code beneath them.

Screen readers read your code.

Blind shoppers use screen readers that work from the page's underlying code. A widget tries to patch that code on the fly, and it can't catch everything.

Lawyers test the real thing.

Plaintiff firms run screen readers and keyboards against your store. If the barrier is there, the claim stands, whatever is installed.

Some fixes need a person.

Writing a useful image description or labeling a control takes judgment. Software can't make that call for every product on your store.

Settlements ask for real fixes.

Settlement agreements in these cases often require actual remediation in the code, and some require the widget to come out.

What the FTC said about accessiBe

The widget industry's central promise is that one line of code makes a site compliant. A federal regulator looked at that promise.

accessiBe settled without admitting wrongdoing. The order binds accessiBe only. Other widget vendors were not part of it.

  1. January 3, 2025

    The FTC announces a $1 million settlement. It says accessiBe's claim that its widget could make any website WCAG-compliant was false, misleading or unsubstantiated.

  2. April 2025

    The FTC approves the final order. accessiBe may not claim its automated products make a site WCAG-compliant without evidence, and must disclose its connections to paid endorsers.

  3. Through 2025

    Lawsuits against sites running widgets keep arriving, month after month.

FAQs

Can I still be sued after Accessrank fixes my site?

Yes. No vendor can promise you won't be sued, and anyone who does is promising something they can't deliver. Fixing the code removes the barriers that complaints are built on, and the Compliance Record documents the work.

Do I have to remove my widget?

No. But once the code is fixed, the widget has little left to patch, and some settlements require its removal. The free check names the widget your store runs, so you can decide.

Isn't a widget cheaper?

The subscription is cheaper. The expense comes if you're sued anyway: legal fees, a settlement, and fixing the code you'd have had to fix regardless.

What does Accessrank cost?

There are four plans. The free check places your store on one, based on your theme and how many apps you run. You see it before you commit to anything.

Will the fixes change how my store looks?

Most fixes happen in the code underneath, where shoppers never see them. Anything visual needs your approval first.

Does this help my search and AI rankings?

Yes. The same fixes make your store easier for Google and AI assistants to read. Accessrank then rebuilds the product data they rank on, and tracks how often AI assistants mention you.

Is there a tax credit for this?

Some small businesses can claim the IRS Disabled Access Credit (Form 8826) for accessibility work. Ask your tax advisor whether your store qualifies.

Do you work with stores outside the US?

No. Accessrank covers US stores only, and works to the ADA.
